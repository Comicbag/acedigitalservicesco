Aftercare

Looking after a new tattoo

The aftercare we give our own clients, written for Wilmington's humidity, sun, and saltwater. If your artist told you something different, follow your artist.

Everything on this page is the aftercare we actually give people at the shop. If your artist told you something different for your specific piece, go with what your artist said. They saw the tattoo, this page did not.

Why aftercare matters

A fresh tattoo is an open wound, and how you treat it in the first couple of weeks decides how it looks for the rest of your life. Good aftercare prevents infection, keeps scabbing to a minimum, and stops the linework going patchy. In Wilmington that matters a little more than it would somewhere dry, because our coastal humidity gives bacteria a friendlier ride and makes it easy to end up with a tattoo that is either too wet or too irritated.

Listen to your artist first

Every artist has their own routine, and they know their own work best. If you get five tattoos you will probably collect five slightly different sets of instructions, and none of them are wrong. If you are already heavily tattooed you have likely worked out what your skin likes, and that is worth more than any generic list.

We treat all of this as an open conversation. Run your usual routine past us and we will tell you honestly whether it suits the piece you just got. Ask questions. Nobody here is going to be short with you for wanting to get it right.

The routine, step by step

Keep it clean

Wash the tattoo gently with lukewarm water and a fragrance free soap, something like Dial or Cetaphil, two or three times a day. Use your hand rather than a cloth. Pat it dry with a clean paper towel instead of rubbing, and skip the bath towel entirely, because towels that live in a bathroom hold onto bacteria.

Moisturise, but do not drown it

A thin layer of a tattoo ointment such as Aquaphor, or an unscented lotion such as Lubriderm, is all it needs. Thin is the important word. In Wilmington's humidity it is genuinely easy to overdo this, and too much product clogs the skin and traps moisture against a healing wound. You want the tattoo supple, not shiny.

Stay out of the sun

Keep a new tattoo out of direct sunlight for at least two to three weeks. UV fades ink fast and it irritates skin that is still repairing itself. This is the rule people here break most, because the beach is right there. Once it is fully healed, a high SPF sunscreen over the tattoo is the single best thing you can do to keep it looking sharp for years.

Skip the ocean and the pool

No submerging the tattoo for at least two weeks. That means no Wrightsville Beach, no pools, no hot tubs, no long soaks in the bath. Ocean water carries bacteria and chlorine will pull at the ink. Showers are fine.

Do not pick or scratch

Scabbing and itching are both normal parts of healing. Picking at a scab pulls ink out with it and leaves you with patchy spots that need touching up. If it itches badly, a light layer of lotion and a gentle tap beats scratching.

Wear something loose

Soft, loose, breathable clothing over the area for the first week. Friction is the quiet enemy of a healing tattoo, so think about what you are putting on before a day out on the Riverwalk or a night at a brewery.

Healing in Wilmington specifically

Living on the coast changes the job slightly.

Humidity cuts both ways. It stops your skin drying out, which is helpful, but it also slows healing if the tattoo stays damp. Sweat is the thing to watch. If you have been outside and the area is damp, clean it and let it dry properly.

An outdoor life is most of the reason people move here, and most of the hazards to a new tattoo. Kayaking the Cape Fear, walking the Historic District in August, working in a yard. Dirt, sun, and water are all worth planning around for two weeks.

Ask somebody. If something looks off, call us. We would far rather answer a question about a tattoo that is healing perfectly normally than see one three weeks later that could have been sorted on day four.

Saniderm, and how to use it

Your artist may send you home under Saniderm, which is a thin, breathable, waterproof adhesive bandage that behaves like a second skin over the tattoo. It holds your body's own healing fluid against the piece, keeps dirt and bacteria out, and generally speeds the whole process up.

People like it for three reasons. There is far less mess, so no plasma and ink stains on your sheets. It reduces scabbing and peeling, which means less risk of losing ink. And it keeps sand, humidity, and germs off the tattoo, which matters here more than most places.

Using it

  1. Leave the first piece on for 12 to 24 hours, or however long your artist told you.
  2. Take it off under lukewarm running water, then pat the tattoo dry.
  3. Either apply the second sheet and leave it 3 to 5 days, or stop there and switch to the normal washing and moisturising routine above.
  4. Whenever you remove it, peel slowly and sideways across the skin rather than pulling it straight up.

Two local notes. Saniderm sticks best to clean, dry skin, so blot any sweat before applying. And it is not a swimming pass, so the two week rule on the ocean and pools still stands.

Most shops here send you home with what you need, and we do too. If you run out, the first aid section of CVS or Walgreens carries it, and it is easy to find online.

When to call us

Get in touch if the bandage leaks, tears, or starts causing redness or itching around the edges, or if you are simply unsure what step you are on. Watch for signs of infection as well: spreading redness, real swelling, heat, or any discharge that is not the normal clear or lightly inked fluid of the first day. If you see those, contact us or a healthcare professional straight away rather than waiting it out.
