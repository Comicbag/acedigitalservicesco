Philosophy

Body art is for everyone

We built this shop for the people who normally have to psych themselves up before walking into one.

🤝 Communication first🏳️‍⚧️ Trans friendly💜 Every body

Where this comes from

We have spent a lot of years working with people getting their first tattoo or their first piercing, and the same worries came up again and again. Not worries about the art. Worries about being judged, being handled carelessly, or having to explain their own body to a stranger who was not going to be kind about it.

As members of the LGBTQ+ and racial minority communities ourselves, we have been on the receiving end of that uncertainty about how we look and how we will be treated. So rather than treat those clients as a special case, we built the whole shop around them.

Who we set the shop up for

Specifically, people who have reason to be cautious. That includes clients who do not fit neatly into gender norms, clients of colour, and plus size clients. It also includes anyone who has previously walked into a studio and been made to feel like an inconvenience.

None of this means we are a niche shop. It means the default setting here is respect, and everybody benefits from that, including the person who has never given it a second thought.

Communication is most of the job

Almost every bad tattoo experience we hear about traces back to somebody not saying something. So we talk. Frankly, and early, about body type, about skin tone, and about how a piece is actually going to heal on you rather than how it looked on a stranger online.

That conversation is not a formality. It is where placement gets decided, where a design gets resized, and where we tell you honestly if what you are asking for is going to age badly.

Bodies are different, and that changes the art

We work with anatomical differences rather than pretending they do not exist. Every body has its own curves, its own scars, and its own way of moving, and a design that ignores that is a design that will look wrong the moment you stand up.

We are experienced and comfortable working with pre and post transition bodies, and with differently abled bodies. If there is something about your body you would normally have to brace yourself to explain, you do not have to brace yourself here.

The questions people are often too polite to ask

Two come up constantly, and we are happy to answer both properly.

What happens if my weight changes? Tattoos move with you, and how much depends heavily on placement and on the design itself. We can tell you which spots are more forgiving and how a particular piece is likely to behave, so you can make the choice with real information.

How will this look on my skin tone? Colour theory and healing interact differently on different skin, and some palettes that look brilliant in a photograph simply will not read the same way on you. Rather than let you find that out a year later, we will talk it through and adjust the design so it works on the skin it is actually going on.

What that adds up to

Body art is for everyone. Not as a slogan on a wall, but as a practical commitment: clean work, honest conversation, no assumptions, and an artist who is paying attention to your body rather than to a template.
