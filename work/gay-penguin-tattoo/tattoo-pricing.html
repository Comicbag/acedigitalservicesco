Pricing

What a tattoo actually costs, and why

No price list, because an honest quote depends on the piece. Here is exactly what goes into the number instead.

💬 Free to ask🎨 Per artist pricing📐 Detail over size

Tattoo pricing is the thing people are most confused about and least comfortable asking about. This page is here to clear up some of the mystery, so that when you do ask, you already know what you are hearing.

We are not going to print a price list, because an honest quote depends on the piece. What we can do is explain exactly what goes into the number.

Why the same tattoo costs different amounts around town

Maybe you have come from out of state and you are surprised how reasonable prices are here. Maybe you priced something downtown, walked in here, and heard a different figure for what looks like the same tattoo. Either way, the question is fair: how can the same design vary so wildly?

The first thing worth saying is that most reputable tattoo and piercing shops in Wilmington are not trying to take advantage of anybody. There are exceptions in every trade, but by and large this is a city full of people who care about their work. Prices vary for reasons that are usually pretty boring once you know them.

Where the shop is

A shop in downtown Wilmington, or in any downtown, can be expected to charge somewhat more than a shop further out. The main reason is simply what it costs to keep the doors open. Downtown rent is higher, and that overhead has to come from somewhere, so tattoos and piercings there carry more of it.

That is not a knock on downtown shops. It is just arithmetic, and it is worth knowing before you assume a higher quote means a better or worse artist.

What the shop is for

Shops also differ in what they are trying to be. A studio run primarily as a business, with income as the main goal, will price accordingly. At the other end, an artist owned shop may charge a great deal more because the people working there treat the art as something sacred and personal, and price their time to match.

Neither approach is wrong. They are different philosophies, and they produce very different numbers for the same square inches of skin.

Why two artists in one shop can quote differently

This one surprises people the most. In most shops, including this one, artists set their own prices. So when you bring in an idea, the artist looks at what it will actually take, and prices it themselves.

A newer artist may quote you less, with the shared understanding that the piece might want a touch up or a little reworking somewhere down the line. A senior artist who has spent decades tattooing places a high value on their minutes, and prices accordingly. Both are honest positions, and which one suits you depends on the piece and on what you want out of it.

What actually drives your quote

Once you are talking to a specific artist about a specific tattoo, the number comes from the work involved. Size and placement matter, because skin behaves differently in different spots and some areas are slower going. Detail matters more than size does, so a small, dense, highly detailed piece can take longer than something large and simple. Colour usually adds time over black and grey. And a cover up is close to always more involved than working on clean skin, because the old tattoo has to be designed around rather than ignored.
