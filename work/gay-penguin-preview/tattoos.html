What we mean by custom

Custom means the design starts with your idea, not with a sheet of pre drawn flash. You bring a concept, a reference, a feeling, or a rough sketch on your phone, and one of our artists draws something that belongs to you and nobody else. If you have seen a piece you love on someone else, we will use it as a jumping off point rather than a template, because a tattoo that copies another person's exactly is a tattoo that means less on you.

The Gay Penguin is a small studio on Market St with four artists, so you are working with a person, not a queue. Brian owns the shop and tattoos. Maya and Sabrina tattoo. Rose pierces and tattoos.

The styles we tattoo

Most people arrive knowing the picture they want but not the word for how it should be drawn. Here is the plain version of each style we work in, so you can point at one and say "that."

Fine line

Thin, delicate linework with very little shading. Good for script, small florals, and pieces meant to look quiet on the skin rather than bold.

Color

Full saturated color, from soft pastel washes to bright and punchy. Great when the whole point of the piece is that it pops.

Black and grey

Black ink diluted into a range of greys for depth and shadow. The classic choice for portraits, realism, and anything that wants to look timeless.

Illustrative

Tattoos that look drawn rather than photographed, with visible linework and a storybook or graphic feel. Sits nicely between traditional and realism.

If your idea spans two of these, that is normal and usually fine. A fine line piece with a small pop of color, or an illustrative piece rendered in black and grey, are both regular requests.

Vegan inks, as standard

Every tattoo here is done with vegan inks and supplies. That is not an upgrade you have to ask for or pay extra for, it is simply what the shop stocks. Some tattoo inks and aftercare products contain animal derived ingredients such as bone char, glycerin, or shellac. Ours do not.

Beyond the ethics, it is a practical answer for anyone who keeps a strict vegan household and would rather not think about what is going under their skin. More on how the vegan side of the studio works.

A studio where you can relax

Getting tattooed means sitting still with a stranger for a couple of hours, sometimes with a fair bit of skin showing. That is a lot easier when you are not bracing for a comment.

The Gay Penguin is an openly LGBTQ+ welcoming shop, and that shapes the whole room: no attitude about what you want tattooed, no jokes at your expense, no assumptions about your body or your pronouns. Plenty of our clients say it is the first shop they have been to where they did not have to psych themselves up in the parking lot first. That is the bar we are aiming for.
